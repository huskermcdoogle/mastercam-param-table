    Speed & feed

    Ribbon: Parameter Table tab › Selected ops › Speed & feed

    What this is for

    A calculator that fills itself in from the op you are on. It has two tabs:

    • Speed & feed – SFM and RPM at a diameter, and feed per rev and per minute. Type in any box; the others follow. It warns when the RPM would go past the op's max RPM.
    • Chip thinning – how thick the chip really is with a round insert or an insert with a lead angle, and the feed to program for the chip you want. It can write that feed back to the op.

    When to use it

    • The insert box says 650 SFM and you want to know the RPM at your diameter.
    • You need the feed in inches per minute for a per-minute op.
    • A round insert or a 45° lead insert is rubbing instead of cutting – the chip is thinner than the feed.

    Steps

    Speed & feed tab

    1. Click a cell in the op's row, then click Speed & feed.
    2. The boxes fill in from the op: diameter (where it cuts), surface speed, RPM, feed per rev, feed per minute.
    3. Type a new value in any box. The others work it out: RPM = SFM × 3.82 ÷ diameter (inch), feed per minute = feed per rev × RPM.
    4. Watch the note: it warns when the RPM is above the op's max RPM (max_ss) – the spindle would stop at the max and the real surface speed is lower.

    Chip thinning tab

    1. Click the Chip thinning tab. It fills in from the op and its tool: the insert size (round inserts) or the entering angle (lead-angle inserts), and the depth of cut.
    2. Read the real chip: real chip = feed × sin(entering angle). A round insert's entering angle comes from its size and the depth: the shallower the cut, the thinner the chip.
    3. Type the chip you want. It shows the feed to program.
    4. Click Set this op's feed to write it into the op's feed cell (it turns yellow).

    The insert size and entering angle come from columns on the Tools page – type them there once per tool. The window stays open and follows the op you click.

    What the messages mean

    Above this row's max_ss of 3500 RPM - at this diameter the spindle stops at ...
    The RPM you asked for is more than the op allows. The machine will run at the max, so the surface speed will be lower than you typed.
    At half the insert or deeper the chip is as thick as the feed - no thinning.
    A round insert cutting half its size deep (or more) has no chip thinning. Program the feed you want as the chip.
    Type the insert diameter and the depth (or stepover) it cuts at.
    The calculator needs both to work out the chip.
    Refused - ... is outside this feed's limits
    The feed it worked out is past what the op takes. Nothing was written.
